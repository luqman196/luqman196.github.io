📋 DSCR Assessment

EBITDA-based DSCR is comfortably above the 1.25x benchmark in every period: 3.19x (FY2023), 1.65x (FY2024) and 2.42x (FY2025). All periods use the same basis (disclosed current portions of term facilities: RM359,571 FY2023, RM802,068 FY2024, RM676,591 FY2025 - hire purchase included) plus total finance costs read directly from the P&L. The FY2024 dip to 1.65x reflected the step-up in term borrowings (loan drawdown of RM3.70m) that lifted current instalments and interest to RM1.13m of debt service. IMPORTANT - EARLY SETTLEMENT: the RHB term loan fell from RM1,169,835 (FY2024) to nil in FY2025 against a disclosed FY2024 current portion of only RM125,477, i.e. approximately RM1,044,358 was repaid ahead of schedule; the cash flow shows total actual loan repayment of RM1,641,862 (versus scheduled term principal of RM676,591). This one-off early settlement is NOT loaded into the coverage ratio - DSCR uses scheduled current portions (2.42x). The settlement should be confirmed with the lender. Note that cash-based coverage tells a different story: reported operating cash flow was negative in FY2024 and FY2025, so cash DSCR was -2.11x and -1.22x; on a normalised basis (excluding the RM1.04m development cost and RM428k gold investment booked within FY2025 operating cash flow) FY2025 normalised cash DSCR is a positive but thin 0.24x. The divergence between strong EBITDA coverage and weak cash coverage is driven by heavy capex/investment and a receivables build, and is the key monitoring point. TWO FURTHER EARLY SETTLEMENTS IN FY2024: the BIMB term loan was cleared in full (RM174,115 repaid against a disclosed FY2023 current portion of RM38,462, RM135,653 ahead of schedule) and the SME Bank term loan likewise (RM166,431 against RM38,461, RM127,970 ahead). Total repaid ahead of schedule across FY2024-FY2025 is therefore RM1,307,981. The two events are different in kind and should be read differently: FY2024 was a REFINANCING - RM3.70m was drawn across three new lenders (AmBank Islamic RM913,583, Bank Muamalat RM951,128, Standard Chartered RM1,538,095) while BIMB and SME Bank were retired, so old facilities were replaced rather than simply repaid. FY2025 was a DELEVERAGING - the RHB settlement of RM1,169,835 came with NO new drawdown and was funded from cash reserves, the released fixed deposits and the new overdraft, which is what drove cash from RM3.09m to RM0.53m alongside RM2.65m of capex. Confirm with each lender whether the settlements were voluntary or lender-driven; a facility not renewed reads very differently from one refinanced on better terms.

📊 Key Observations

Revenue Trend

Revenue grew strongly from RM21.10m (FY2023) to RM31.47m (FY2024) and RM49.01m (FY2025), a 132% increase over two years driven by vessel maintenance, chartering, sale of vessels and manpower supply. The FY2025 jump was underpinned by higher subcontract activity (RM14.85m) and material purchases (RM9.29m), pointing to larger project scale.

Profitability Trend

Despite top-line momentum, margins compressed each year: gross margin fell from 19.51% (FY2023) to 11.25% (FY2025) as vessel charter (RM12.66m) and subcontract costs outpaced revenue, and net margin narrowed from 3.91% to 2.10%. Absolute net profit still rose from RM824,795 to RM1,031,078, so the business remains profitable but is becoming lower-margin.

Liquidity Position

The current ratio held around 2.2x in FY2023-FY2024 before falling to 1.51x in FY2025 - still above the 1.25x benchmark. The cash ratio, however, collapsed from 0.86x to 0.10x as cash and bank fell from RM3.09m to RM0.53m and a RM785,450 overdraft appeared, signalling tightening liquidity.

Working Capital Cycle

The cash conversion cycle turned from -26.72 days (FY2023) to +19.39 days (FY2025), and OWC swung from -RM910,413 to +RM2,888,289, as receivables climbed to RM5.13m while trade payables shrank to RM2.25m (creditor days 56 to 19). The company has moved from being supplier-funded to needing external working-capital support of roughly RM2.60m.

Debt Structure

Total interest-bearing borrowings were RM4.13m at FY2025 (gearing 0.89x), comprising term loans from AmBank Islamic (RM797,299), Standard Chartered (RM1,295,238) and Bank Muamalat (RM838,242), hire purchase (RM413,107) and a new RHB overdraft (RM785,450). Gearing peaked at 1.42x in FY2024 after a RM3.70m drawdown, then eased in FY2025 partly via the early full settlement of the RHB term loan. Across FY2023-FY2025 five term facilities turned over: BIMB and SME Bank were settled in FY2024 against a RM3.70m drawdown from three new lenders (a refinancing), and RHB was settled in FY2025 with no new borrowing (a deleveraging funded from liquidity). RM1,307,981 was repaid ahead of contractual schedule over the two years.

Funding Position

Long-term funding (equity RM4.66m plus non-current liabilities RM2.83m = RM7.49m) comfortably exceeds non-current assets of RM4.78m in FY2025, leaving a matched/sustainable structure with a negative funding gap of RM2.72m (-56.9% of NCA). All three periods are matched, so long-term assets are not being financed by short-term money.

Asset Base

Non-current assets expanded to RM4.78m in FY2025 from RM1.34m, driven by RM2.62m of machinery additions, a RM1.04m building under construction and a RM428,370 Bank Islam gold investment. This capital deepening explains the surge in depreciation to RM680,925 and the heavy investing outflow of RM2.65m.

Related Party Exposure

Advances to directors of RM876,661 in FY2024 reduced to RM470,332 in FY2025; these are unsecured and repayable on demand. Advances from directors of RM61,288 in FY2023 were repaid. The net director exposure remains a governance monitoring item but is modest against equity of RM4.66m.
